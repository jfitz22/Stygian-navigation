LW-0 ORIENTATION

Somewhere in this sea drifts Elgarz, the glacier that swallowed Citadel Coldsteel. Find it, prove it, and strike it with a green beacon. The ice will answer.

How a watch goes

A suggested order of operations. Nothing stops you doing it differently.

  1. Start up. Light the furnace; fill the fuel chute from the rune board; stoke (LW-1, LW-2). The wire service shows a startup checklist.
  2. Look. Power ORBS, SONAR and CURRENTS (three at most). Watch the orbs for big ice. Drop a buoy near ice and PING once.
  3. Sift. Click through the contacts. Each printout gives length (20 or under: out) and the echo. You want HALLS; mind cold water (LW-3). Orbs give length too (LW-4).
  4. Lock and aim. From the sonar (the buoy reads the current; every ping refreshes it) or from an orb (hold the ice in view and the orb measures its drift: no buoy). LW-5.
  5. Beacon it. A red beacon pins the ice to the case board (LW-9), lets the radio hear it (LW-6), and the ice reports its own position from then on: every later shot at it has full aim.
  6. Prove it. Radio for the Triad (LW-6). Scanner for metal, with the buoy beside the ice (LW-7). Outside the Tomb's ring?
  7. Accuse. Fire green only when everything agrees. There are .
Mind the hunters. Every ping calls the Grindmaw; every buoy splashdown calls Old Tom; any beacon that hits a frozen monster lets it out. All of them go for the buoy. Ping, then move it.

LW-3 SONAR & ECHOES

ASK THE OPERATOR: for each contact: its length · the WATER temperature on the strip · the bumps (even or uneven?) · the tail (flat, wavy or pulsing?)

Drop a buoy, then PING once. Every contact in range comes back with a full printout; click each one to read it. With CURRENTS powered, a ping also takes a current reading. Contacts fade after s.

The printout is live: it sweeps across the strip every few seconds. Watch two sweeps. The first spike is the surface (taller = longer; 20 miles or under is not Elgarz). Then one bump per chamber. Then the tail. A wavy tail ripples the same way every sweep; a pulsing tail beats in pairs that swell and fade from sweep to sweep.

Cold water. Below ° on the strip, a frozen monster is too cold to pulse and reads exactly like HALLS. In cold water, treat HALLS as a possible monster until the radio or scanner says otherwise.

Frozen monsters: hit one with any beacon and it wakes and swims for the buoy at the Grindmaw's pace. It eats a buoy it reaches. Move the buoy a full buoy range away and it sinks away. The ice it leaves is empty caverns.

LW-3A HUNTERS & SHOALS

ASK THE OPERATOR: where the Grindmaw and Old Tom are, and where they are heading

The Grindmaw answers every ping: it races to the spot (fast while far, slow within about a square and a half), eats a buoy it finds there, then circles the Watch. Old Tom wakes after about ten minutes and does the same for every buoy splashdown, circling the other way. A lost buoy tears the winch: repair it (LW-8).

Shoals

Four shoals, hatched on the chart. Rock scatters the sonar: nothing inside a shoal echoes, and a buoy on the rocks hears nothing. Look in with the orbs; print an echo with an orange charge.

Frozen monsters (LW-3) swim for the buoy like the others, but give up if the buoy moves a full buoy range away.

LW-5 AIMING & BEACONS

ASK THE OPERATOR: the AIM QUALITY number · which bar is low · the drift switch

A beacon flies to the PREDICTED position: the last fix, moved by either the buoy's current reading and the drift switch, or an orb's drift measurement. AIM QUALITY multiplies three bars:

FIG. 5.1 Each bar stays full to the marked point, then falls to nothing. With an orb track, the TRACK bar replaces the two reading bars.
  • Green lamp (70%+): should hit. Amber: maybe. Red: save the beacon.
  • Aim quality cannot see the drift switch. Large ice: DEEP. Small ice: SURFACE. An orb track ignores the switch.
  • Ice that already carries a beacon reports its own position and drift: aim is 100%, nothing else needed.
  • Every miss is reported with its distance and reasons; the chart marks where the target really was.
  • The launcher jams every few shots (repair, LW-8).

What a beacon does on impact

Every hit pins the ice to the case board (LW-9) and lets the radio hear it (LW-6). Any hit on a frozen monster lets it out (LW-3).

LW-9 THE CASE BOARD

ASK THE OPERATOR: the rows on the case board and what each cell shows

Top right of the main board. Locking ice gives it a dim temporary row; a beacon hit pins it. Readings fill in as they are taken, including readings from before it was pinned. Clicking a row re-locks that ice: live if beaconed, otherwise from where it was last seen. If the ice carries a blue beacon, its drift log shows on the chart while selected.
