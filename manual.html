Your mission

Somewhere in this sea drifts Elgarz, the glacier that swallowed Citadel Coldsteel. Find it, prove it, and strike it with a green beacon. When a green beacon hits Elgarz, the ice answers with a blaze of blue light, and the watch is done.

No single sign is proof. About a third of all ice is riddled with caves and rings hollow. Several icebergs sing the Triad: Geryon's herd left relics all over this sea. Plenty of ice has metal frozen inside. At least one iceberg has the first three signs and is drawn toward the Tomb. Elgarz may not even be in the sea when your watch begins.

The machine in one minute

  1. Light the furnace and keep it fed (page 3). It runs at most three systems: Cameras, Sonar, Radio, Scanner, Currents.
  2. Find ice with the cameras or the sonar. Click a contact or an iceberg on camera to lock onto it. A camera that keeps locked ice in view measures its drift, so you can hit it without a buoy.
  3. Test the locked iceberg with the echo printout, radio and scanner.
  4. Look up to the overhead deck to fire a beacon. Tagged ice stays marked on the chart, so you can watch it drift.

The operator gets tired

As the watch goes on, the screens darken in from the edges, blur and sway, and the operator starts to blink off. The eye on the top bar shows how tired they are (ALERT, TIRED, DROWSY, NODDING OFF). Coffee fixes it. Brew a pot from the rune board (it costs a little furnace heat), then click the mug on the top bar to drink. Each pot is sips.

Cabin lighting

The LAMPS rune cycles the cabin lighting: normal, night (the board goes dark and only the screens glow) and red (everything turns red, which makes the radio lamps hard to read). Press it again to move on.

Sonar, the echo and the Grindmaw

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: which square the buoy is in · how many contacts came back · where the Grindmaw is
  1. Drop a buoy (DROP BUOY, then click inside the dashed line), then PING. The echo comes back in seconds.
  2. Contacts show where the ice was at the ping. They last seconds. Ping the same ice again and a dotted trail joins the contacts, showing which way it is going.
  3. Every echo also draws current arrows round the buoy for a while: purple for DEEP, blue for SURFACE.

The Grindmaw

A colossal Styx shark, always on the chart. Every ping calls it to the spot where you pinged. While it is far away it races; within about one and a half squares it slows to a steady swim. If the buoy is still there when it arrives, it eats the buoy and tears the winch cable. Repair the buoy winch before you can launch another buoy. Ping, then move the buoy.

Reading the echo printout

Ignore the first tall spike (the surface). Count the rounded bumps after it, then look at the tail.

Bumps with a wavy tail that fades: HOLLOW, one bump per chamber. Many icebergs are hollow, so a hollow echo puts ice on your shortlist; it does not prove anything. Bumps with a scratchy tail: soft walls, something alive. Not a building. No bumps but scratchy: rotten slush.

Big ice or small ice?

The printout gives the length. Over 8 miles is LARGE and rides the DEEP current. 8 or less is SMALL and rides the SURFACE and the wind. Set the drift switch to match.

The lock, aim quality and the beacon

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: the AIM QUALITY number · which of its three bars is low · the drift switch setting

The beacon does not fly at the iceberg. It flies at the PREDICTED circle: where the machine thinks the ice will be when the beacon lands. The prediction uses the last fix (a sonar contact or a camera sighting), the current reading at the buoy and the drift switch. The circle grows as the prediction gets less certain.

Green lamp (70%+): it should hit. Amber: maybe. Red: save your beacon. Aim quality cannot see the drift switch. If the switch is wrong for the ice's size, even 100% misses.

Camera tracking: aiming without a buoy

Lock onto an iceberg and keep it in the camera on screen. After about seconds the feed says TRACKING ✓ DRIFT MEASURED: the camera has measured how the ice is really moving. The prediction then uses that measurement instead of the buoy, so no buoy and no drift switch are needed. Aim quality shows a CAMERA TRACK bar that ages once the ice leaves the view. Snow on the lens stops tracking.

When a shot misses

The launcher prints how far it missed by and why, and the chart shows a dotted line from the splash to where the target really was. Read it, fix it, fire again.

The launcher jams

Every few shots the launcher jams. Repair it in the repair bay.

The radio

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: the frequency · the wave shape · the three lamp colours, left to right · is the wireless on?

1. Tune

Lock onto an iceberg. Slide the frequency along the band and watch the SIGNAL bar. About half the ice makes some sound.

2. Match the gain

Move the GAIN until the wave just touches the dashed brass lines; then three lamps flash. Too little and it drowns in static. Too much and the CLIP lamp lights. Leave it clipping for a few seconds and the fuse blows (repair bay). Distant ice needs more gain.

3. Is it the cabin wireless?

While the WIRELESS rune has it playing, three stations from Dis are on the band. They sound just like ice, and one sounds like the Triad. Turn the wireless off. If the signal stays, it is real.

4. Read the lamps by the wave shape

JAGGED
Left to right, but red means blue and blue means red.

5. Look it up by band

LOW is below 400, MID is 400–699, HIGH is 700 and up. The band shows in the corner of the radio screen.

Elgarz sings THE TRIAD, and so do some others. The Triad narrows the search. It does not end it.

Burrowers (remorhazes)

  • A camera heats up fast while you watch it (heat bar on its button). A hot camera draws a remorhaz; the button says SHAKING.
  • Switch to another camera and let it cool, and the beast burrows away. If it arrives, the camera is destroyed.

Storms

Storms cross the sea often. A camera inside one is snowed out. The WIPERS rune clears the lens for a few seconds.
