Your mission

Somewhere in this sea drifts Elgarz, the glacier that swallowed Citadel Coldsteel. Find it, prove it, and strike it with a green beacon. When a green beacon hits Elgarz, the ice will answer with a blaze of blue light, and the watch is done.

Other icebergs show one or two of these signs, and some show three. Some are very convincing. Only Elgarz has everything. Elgarz may not be in the sea when your watch begins.

The machine in one minute

  1. Light the furnace (bottom left) and keep it fed (page 2).
  2. The furnace runs at most three systems at once: Cameras, Sonar, Radio, Scanner, Currents.
  3. Find ice with the cameras or the sonar. Click a sonar contact or an iceberg on camera to lock onto it.
  4. Test the locked iceberg with the echo printout, radio and scanner.
  5. Fire a beacon to tag it with a colour. Tagged ice stays marked on the chart, so you can watch where it drifts.

The chart

The Last Watch stands on an island in the middle of the sea. The chart is cut into squares, lettered A to L across and numbered 1 to 12 down, so F7 is just south-west of the island. Each square is miles. Call positions by square. The shaded corners are beyond your reach.

The furnace and power

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: where the HEAT needle is on the gauge · how many systems are in use

Everything runs on the furnace. It burns down all the time. Feed it with STOKE: each shovel takes a moment to catch.

A shovel adds about heat. Two shovels from the bottom of the green band is safe. Three is not. Left alone, the furnace drops out of the green band in about two minutes.

Power

  • Cameras: the camera posts.
  • Sonar: needed to drop a buoy and to ping.
  • Currents: reads wind, current and water temperature at the buoy.
  • Radio and Scanner: test a locked iceberg.

A system takes a few seconds to warm up when switched on. Switching systems on and off costs nothing, so juggle them.

Sonar, buoys and the Grindmaw

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: which square the buoy is in · how many contacts came back · where the Grindmaw is
  1. Sonar needs a buoy in the water. Press DROP BUOY, then click inside the dashed launcher line on the chart.
  2. Press PING. The echo takes seconds to come back.
  3. Contacts appear on the sonar and the chart as green dots. They fade after seconds.
A contact shows where the ice WAS when you pinged, not where it is now. The ice keeps drifting. Lock onto fresh contacts.

The Grindmaw

A colossal Styx shark, marked in red on the chart at all times. Every ping calls it.

  • After a ping it swims straight for the spot where you last pinged. A dashed red line shows its course. It is slow, but it never stops.
  • When it gets there, if the buoy is still there, the buoy is eaten. A new one takes seconds to build.
  • Ping again somewhere else and it turns toward the new spot.
  • Save the buoy: move it before the Grindmaw arrives. Ping when the shark is far away.

Reading the echo printout

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: how many bumps after the first tall spike · what the line does at the end (wavy and fading, scratchy, or flat)

Every printout starts with one tall spike. That is the surface of the ice. Ignore it. Count the rounded bumps after it, then look at the tail.

No bumps: SOLID ice. Not hollow.

Bumps with a wavy tail that fades away (it rings): HOLLOW. Each bump is a chamber inside. More bumps, more rooms.

Bumps with a scratchy, ragged tail: SOFT WALLS. Something alive. This is not a hollow building. Leave it alone.

No bumps but a scratchy tail: rotten, slushy ice. Not hollow.

Currents, the lock and the beacon

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: how old the fix is · which square the buoy reading came from · which square the prediction is in

When the operator locks onto an iceberg, the chart shows a white dashed circle marked PREDICTED. That is where the machine thinks the ice has drifted since the fix.

The prediction uses the Currents panel and the drift switch:

  • Currents are measured at the buoy. The Currents system must be on, and it needs a buoy in the water.
  • The sea swirls. A reading taken far from your target is wrong for your target. Put the buoy close to the ice you care about.
  • Choose DEEP for large ice and SURFACE for small ice (page 3).
  • With no reading at all, the prediction does not move.

The scanner needle and the radio signal both weaken as the prediction drifts away from the real ice. If they fade, take a fresh fix: ping again and lock again.

Firing a beacon

The beacon flies from the observatory and lands where the prediction says the ice will be when it arrives (about miles per second).

For a hit you need: a fresh lock (ping, lock, fire quickly), a recent current reading taken near the target, and the right drift setting. A miss falls into the sea.

Every hit tags the iceberg on the chart in the beacon's colour, so you can watch it drift.

The radio

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: the frequency · the shape of the wave · the three lamp colours, left to right · is the MUSIC on?

1. Tune

Lock onto an iceberg. The radio listens to it. Slide the frequency slowly along the band and watch the SIGNAL bar. Many icebergs make some sound. Some are silent.

2. Match the gain

When the signal is strong, move the GAIN until the wave just touches the two dashed brass lines. Too little and it drowns in static. Too much and the CLIP lamp lights. The further away the ice, the more gain it needs. When the gain is right, three lamps flash.

3. Is it the cabin wireless?

The cabin MUSIC is a wireless set. While it plays, three stations from Dis are on the band. They sound just like ice. One of them even sounds like the Triad. Before you believe any signal, turn the music OFF. If the signal is still there, it is real.

4. Read the lamps by the wave shape

SMOOTH
Read the lamps as shown, left to right.
STEPPED
Read the lamps right to left.
JAGGED
Read left to right, but every red is really blue and every blue is really red.

5. Look it up by band

LOW is below 400. MID is 400 to 699. HIGH is 700 and above. The band is printed in the corner of the radio screen.

Elgarz sings THE TRIAD. No other ice does.

Cameras, burrowers and repairs

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: which camera buttons say SHAKING · the heat bars

There are seven camera posts. Each one is fixed and always looks the same way. Ice drifts through their view. Click an iceberg in the view to lock onto it.

Burrowers (remorhazes)

  • A camera heats up while you watch it, and fast. Its heat bar is on its button.
  • A hot camera draws a remorhaz across the ice. The button says SHAKING. Switch to that camera and you will see it coming.
  • Switch to another camera and let it cool. Cameras cool quickly, and a cold camera is not worth the trip, so the beast burrows away.
  • If it arrives, the camera is destroyed.

Storms

Storms cross the sea. A camera inside one is snowed out. The WIPERS clear the lens for a few seconds.

Cabin controls

The coffee, wipers, music, lamps and bell are along the top. They move around. Nobody knows why. Remember: the music puts false stations on the radio (page 6).
