Orientation

Somewhere in this sea drifts Elgarz, the glacier that swallowed Citadel Coldsteel. Find it, prove it, and strike it with a green beacon. When a green beacon hits Elgarz, the ice answers with a blaze of blue light, and the watch is done.

How a watch goes

A suggested order. Nothing stops you doing it differently.

  1. Start up. Light the furnace, fill the fuel chute from the rune board and keep it fed (pages 3–4). The wire service on the overhead deck shows a startup checklist.
  2. Look. Power the orbs, the sonar and the currents (only three at once). Watch the orbs for big ice. Drop a buoy near ice and PING once.
  3. Sift. Click through the contacts one by one. Each printout gives the length (20 or under: out) and the echo. You want HALLS. Mind the water temperature (page 5). An orb also gives the length of ice you lock.
  4. Lock and aim. Lock from the sonar (the buoy's current reading does the aiming) or from an orb (keep the ice in view a few seconds while the orb measures its drift). Either works. The launcher shows your hit chance. Page 6.
  5. Beacon it. A red beacon pins the ice to the case board, lets the radio hear it, and from then on the ice reports its own position, so every later shot at it has full aim.
  6. Prove it. Radio: tune to it and decode the lamps (page 7). Scanner: drop the buoy beside it and scan for metal (page 8). Check it is outside the Tomb's ring.
  7. Accuse. When everything agrees, fire green. You have .

Meanwhile: every ping calls the Grindmaw and every buoy splashdown calls Old Tom (page 5). Ping, then move the buoy.

The Last Watch stands on an island in the middle of the sea. Squares are lettered A–L across and numbered 1–12 down; each is miles. Call positions by square. The shaded corners are beyond your reach.

The operator gets tired

As the watch goes on, the screens darken in from the edges, blur and sway, and the operator starts to blink off. The eye on the top bar shows how tired they are (ALERT, TIRED, DROWSY, NODDING OFF). Coffee fixes it. Brew a pot from the rune board (it costs a little furnace heat), then click the mug on the top bar to drink. Each pot is sips.

Cabin lighting

The LIGHTS rune cycles the cabin lighting: normal, then red emergency lighting (the radio lamps become hard to read), then green night lighting, then normal again. Find it on the board and press it until the lights are back.

Sonar, echoes and the hunters

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: which square the buoy is in · how many contacts came back · for each contact: its length, the WATER temperature on the strip, the bumps, and what the tail does
  1. Drop a buoy (DROP BUOY, then click inside the dashed line), then PING (or the space bar). The echo comes back in seconds. If Currents is powered, the ping also takes a current reading.
  2. Contacts show where the ice was at the ping. They last seconds. Click each one to print its echo and lock onto it. One ping is enough to read every contact.

Reading the printout

The printout is a live trace: it sweeps across the strip every few seconds, over and over. Watch at least two sweeps. From left to right:

  • The first tall spike is the surface. Taller means longer ice. The length is printed above it. 20 miles or under: not Elgarz.
  • Then one bump per chamber. Are the bumps even (same height, same spacing) or uneven?
  • Then the tail: flat, wavy (steady ripples, the same every sweep), or pulsing (little double beats that swell and fade from one sweep to the next).

Frozen monsters

Something sleeps in some of the ice. Hit it with any beacon and it wakes: it bursts out and swims straight for the buoy, as fast as the Grindmaw. If it reaches the buoy it eats it and tears the winch. Move the buoy at least one buoy range away (the green circle) and it loses interest and sinks away. The ice it left behind is just empty caverns.

Shoals

Four rocky shoals are hatched on the chart. Rock scatters the sonar: nothing inside a shoal echoes, and a buoy dropped on the rocks hears nothing at all. Use the scrying orbs to look in (each shoal sits beside an orb, just out of its starting view) and an orange charge to print an echo.

The Grindmaw

A colossal Styx shark, always on the chart. Every ping calls it to the spot where you pinged. While it is far away it races; within about one and a half squares it slows to a steady swim. If the buoy is still there when it arrives, it eats the buoy and tears the winch cable. Repair the buoy winch before you can launch another buoy. Once it reaches the spot, it circles the Last Watch at that distance until the next ping. Ping, then move the buoy.

Old Tom

After about ten minutes something else wakes. Old Tom, marked orange on the chart, swims to wherever a buoy last splashed down, as fast as the Grindmaw. It eats a buoy it finds there, then circles the Watch the other way until the next splashdown. A buoy dropped far away makes a fine decoy. After ten minutes the storms come more often too.

Big ice or small ice?

Ice over 8 miles long rides the deep current; smaller ice rides the surface and the wind. The machine picks the right one by itself when it aims.

The lock, the hit chance and the beacon

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: the HIT CHANCE · the line written under it · where the fix came from (SONAR, ORB or BEACON)

Lock onto ice from the sonar (click a contact) or from an orb (click the ice in the view). Either works. The beacon flies to where the machine predicts the ice will be when it lands, and the launcher shows the HIT CHANCE. That number is honest: a 90% shot hits nine times in ten.

What makes the chance high

THE FIX CAME FROMWHAT IT NEEDS
SONARThe Currents system powered, with the buoy close to the ice. Every ping takes a fresh reading. The fix ages: after about a minute, ping again.
ORBKeep the ice in the orb's view for about seconds. The feed says TRACKING ✓ DRIFT MEASURED, and the chance climbs to the top. If the orb loses sight of the ice, the chance falls again. Snow on the lens stops tracking.

When the chance is low, a line under it says what is wrong and what to do. Faster-drifting ice is harder to hit: fire soon after the fix.

The radio

ASK THE OPERATOR FOR: the frequency · the wave shape · the three lamp colours, left to right · can you hear music from the cabin radio?

1. Tune

The radio is on the overhead deck. It does not listen for transmitters; it picks up the energies the ice gives off, and it can only hear them through a beacon lodged in the ice, which amplifies them. Beacon the ice first (any colour), then select it on the case board or lock onto it, slide the frequency along the band and watch the SIGNAL bar. About half the ice gives off something. What it means is up to this book.

2. Match the gain

Move the GAIN until the wave just touches the dashed brass lines; then three lamps flash. Too little and it drowns in static. Too much and the CLIP lamp lights. Leave it clipping and the FUSE bar fills; when it flashes red the fuse is about to blow (repair bay). Distant ice needs more gain.

3. Is it the cabin radio?

While the RADIO rune has the cabin radio playing (you will hear the music), three stations from Dis are on the band. They look just like ice, and one sounds like the Triad. Turn the cabin radio off. If the signal stays, it is real.

4. Read the lamps by the wave shape

SMOOTH
As shown, left to right.
STEPPED
Right to left.
JAGGED
Left to right, but red means blue and blue means red.

5. Look it up by band

LOW is below 400, MID is 400–699, HIGH is 700 and up. The band shows in the corner of the radio screen.

Elgarz sings THE TRIAD, and so do some others. The Triad narrows the search. It does not end it.

Burrowers (remorhazes)

  • An orb heats up fast while you watch it (heat bar on its button). A hot orb draws a remorhaz; the button says SHAKING.
  • Switch to another orb and let it cool, and the beast burrows away. If it arrives, the orb is destroyed.

Storms

Storms cross the sea often. An orb inside one is snowed out until the storm moves on. Use another orb meanwhile.

The case board

The case board, top right of the main board, keeps track of the ice you have looked at.

  • Lock onto an iceberg and it gets a dim, temporary row.
  • Hit it with a beacon (any colour) and the row is pinned. Readings taken earlier are kept.
  • Click a row to lock onto that ice again: from its live position if it carries a beacon, otherwise from where it was last seen.
  • The row of the ice you are locked on glows. If it carries a blue beacon, its drift log shows on the chart.

The password

The Watch has one password. When it is needed, a terminal replaces the chart, and the furnace controls and the launcher show LOCKED until it is entered. The sea does not wait: the furnace burns and the hunters keep coming.

WHENWHAT IT WANTS

If there is no password yet, the first lock asks you to set one. Write it down. Unlocking needs it exactly, capitals and all.

Five wrong tries and the system reboots. The furnace goes out, the fuel chute empties, every system switches off and one or two things break. Beacons already fired stay where they are. Then a fresh password must be set.

The rules

A new password must obey every rule in force. The terminal lists them and ticks each one as it is met. The first password needs rules 1 to 3; each lockdown adds two more.
