LW-0 ORIENTATION

The Last Watch is an outpost of the True Navy of the Fifth, still loyal to Geryon. Levistus has hunted for Geryon a long time. What he seeks drifts somewhere in this sea: Elgarz, a glacier among thousands, known to the Navy only by a set of coded signs.

When the Navy must speak with Elgarz, this outpost makes contact. Find the glacier that carries the signs, then strike it with a green transmitter beacon. Elgarz will recognise the call and answer, revealing its position for a time.

This manual is issued in three books: ENGINEERING, GUNNERY and SIGNALS. No officer holds every procedure, so most tasks need two of you. Where a page says ASK SIGNALS, ask Signals.

LW-0B THE SEA

The water of Stygia runs from about −185° to −255°, near −215° on a usual night: coldest near the Tomb, and colder as the night goes on. Things live in it. All of them go for the buoy. Ping, then move the buoy.

On the cold. Nothing can be colder than absolute zero: −273.15° (0 K; −459.67° on the old Fahrenheit scale). The ice by the Tomb comes within twenty degrees of it. Nowhere else in the Hells is so near.

The hunters

The Grindmaw answers every ping. It races to the spot (fast from far off, slow over the last square and a half), eats any buoy it finds there, then circles the Watch. Old Tom wakes after about ten minutes and does the same for every buoy splashdown, circling the other way. Frozen monsters sleep in some ice; a beacon strike wakes one, and it swims for the buoy. Move the buoy a full buoy range away and it loses interest.

FIG. 0.3 The hunters. Each eats a buoy it finds, then circles the Watch: opposite ways.

Weather, rock and burrowers

  • Storms drift across the sea. Snow on an orb blinds it. A buoy left in a storm for about s is torn loose, and the winch with it. Move it out.
  • Shoals: nothing inside one returns an echo, and a buoy on the rocks hears nothing. Use the orbs instead.
  • Remorhazes burrow towards an orb that is watched too long. Look away and it cools. The COOLANT rune cools every orb at once.

Hit chance and beacons

The launcher shows the HIT CHANCE for a beacon fired now at the locked ice. A fresh fix and a good drift measurement keep it high: a buoy reading taken close to the ice, or an unlocked orb tracking it. Wind and snow at an orb cap what its track is worth (never below 50%); a fresh current reading near the ice has no such limit, and the launcher uses whichever is better. Ice already carrying a beacon reports its own position: 100%. When the chance is low, the line beneath it says why.

Red pins the ice to the case board and lets the radio hear it. Orange also prints its echo. Green carries the Navy's call (GU-2), and recovers any infernal artifact the scanner has named in that glacier. New beacons are built in the Gunnery workshop.

LW-0C BREAKDOWNS & THE PASSWORD

What breaks, and whose book mends it

Machines also fail without warning. Every fault shows on the same board in the REPAIR BAY (look up): four conduits, each with three lights, a gauge and a fault code. The flowchart that reads it is in the owner's book. The repair crew needs the REPAIR switch on, except at the furnace grate, which is mended by hand.

The password

The Watch has one password. When it is called for, a terminal replaces the chart and the furnace and launcher lock. The sea does not wait. The Watch leaves the yard on the factory password, JerryRulz!

      EN-7 THE BREAKERS & THE FUSE BOX

      The breaker panel sits on your station at all times: six by six. Pressing a breaker flips it and its four neighbours. Get every breaker dark and the boiler hands the furnace a free shovel of fuel. One free shovel a minute; nothing goes wrong if you leave it.

      The fuse box blows every few minutes, and whenever the furnace runs into the red. Drag each loose wire to the terminal of the same colour and stripe, within 20 seconds.

          SI-6 MINESWEEPING & THE CABLE

          Reading echoes. Click any contact on your copy of the sonar to print its echo. It does not change what the operator has selected or locked: read it, then talk them onto the right ice.

          Minesweeping sits on your station at all times: eight by eight, eight mines, a safe patch already open. Complete a sweep and the Watch's guns are free to fire: a beacon strikes a random iceberg, pinning it to the case board. Or choose to plot an enemy ship instead: its outline appears on the fleet tables. One sweep a minute; a mine costs nothing but the sweep.

          • Left click opens a square. A number is how many of the eight squares around it hold a mine.
          • Right click flags a square you know is a mine. No guessing is ever needed.

          The buoy cable snaps every few minutes. Steer the splice with the arrow keys (or WASD) and collect eight loose ends within 45 seconds. The cable starts fourteen lengths long and grows to twenty-two. Run into a wall and you come out the other side; run into the cable itself and the splice fails.

          If the splice fails, or time runs out, the buoy is lost (the winch must be repaired).
