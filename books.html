LW-0 ORIENTATION

The Last Watch is an outpost of the True Navy of the Fifth, still loyal to Geryon. Levistus has hunted for Geryon a long time. What he seeks drifts somewhere in this sea: Elgarz, a glacier among thousands, known to the Navy only by a set of coded signs.

When the Navy must speak with Elgarz, this outpost makes contact. Find the glacier that carries the signs, then strike it with a green transmitter beacon. Elgarz will recognise the call and answer, revealing its position for a time.

This manual is issued in four books: ENGINEERING, GUNNERY, SIGNALS and FLEET COMMAND. No officer holds every procedure, so most tasks need two of you. Where a page says ASK SIGNALS, ask Signals.

LW-0B THE SEA

The water of Stygia runs from about −185° to −255°, near −215° on a usual night: coldest near the Tomb, and colder as the night goes on. Things live in it. All of them go for the buoy. Ping, then move the buoy.

Nothing can be colder than absolute zero, −273 °C or 0 K. The ice by the Tomb comes within twenty degrees of it.

The hunters

The Grindmaw answers every ping. It races to the spot (fast from far off, slow over the last square and a half), eats any buoy it finds there, then circles the Watch. Old Tom wakes after about ten minutes and does the same for every buoy splashdown, circling the other way. Frozen monsters sleep in some ice; a beacon strike wakes one, and it swims for the buoy. Move the buoy a full buoy range away and it loses interest.

FIG. 0.3 The hunters. Each eats a buoy it finds, then circles the Watch: opposite ways.

Weather, rock and burrowers

  • Storms drift across the sea. Snow on an orb blinds it. A buoy left in a storm for about s is torn loose, and the winch with it. Move it out.
  • Shoals: nothing inside one returns an echo, and a buoy on the rocks hears nothing. Use the orbs instead.
  • Remorhazes burrow towards an orb that is watched too long. Look away and it cools. The COOLANT rune cools every orb at once.

Beacons

Check HIT CHANCE before firing. Gunnery can help improve it (GU-2).

Red pins the ice to the case board and lets the radio hear it. Orange also prints its echo. Green carries the Navy's call (GU-2), and recovers any infernal artifact the scanner has named in that glacier. New beacons are built in the Gunnery workshop; the rack holds red, orange and green at most.

Not all ice is ice. Four devil warships lie disguised among the glaciers (LW-0E): large, hollow and full of metal, and their radio plays war drums.

LW-0C BREAKDOWNS & THE PASSWORD

What breaks, and whose book mends it

Machines also fail without warning. Every fault shows on the same board in the REPAIR BAY (look up): four conduits, each with three lights, a gauge and a fault code. The flowchart that reads it is in the owner's book. The repair crew needs the REPAIR switch on, except at the furnace grate, which is mended by hand.

        SI-5 THE TRANSMITTER CRYSTAL

        ASK GUNNERY: the casing's serial, its lamp, and the call band from their codebook

        Every green beacon carries a transmitter crystal, cut to Signals' design: it is what carries the Navy's call to Elgarz. Gunnery fits it at the bench; this book says which crystal. A crystal is told by its facets (three, five or seven) and its tint (clear, smoky or rose).

          Example. Serial KTR-47, lamp yellow, call band MID: five facets; the letters are all different and the lamp is yellow, so smoky.

          SI-6 MINESWEEPING & THE CABLE

          Minesweeping sits on your station at all times: eight by eight, eight mines, a safe patch already open. Complete a sweep and the Watch's guns are free to fire: a beacon strikes a random iceberg, pinning it to the case board. Or choose to sight a square of an enemy hull on the fleet tables. One sweep a minute; a mine costs nothing but the sweep.

          The fleet. Your special is the sounding: how many ship squares lie in one row or column. It starts loaded. To reload, describe your four flags; the Fleet Officer names each rune by house and weight; find it in your rune catalogue (SI-1) and press it. Every second reload a Morse question follows: read it out, choose their answer.

          • Left click opens a square. A number is how many of the eight squares around it hold a mine.
          • Right click flags a square you know is a mine. No guessing is ever needed.

          The buoy cable snaps every few minutes. Steer the splice with the arrow keys (or WASD) and collect ten loose ends within 45 seconds. The cable starts fifteen lengths long and grows to twenty-five; the first five seconds are safe. Run into a wall and you come out the other side; run into the cable itself, or one of the stray sparks drifting through the water, and the splice fails. A loose end left for six seconds sinks, and another floats up somewhere else.

          If the splice fails, or time runs out, the buoy is lost (the winch must be repaired).
